# Waite Tarot Learning Skill

Teach the Rider-Waite-Smith system as a visual language rather than a list of memorised keywords. Let the learner observe first; then distinguish their intuitive reading from interpretations supported by the historical reference.

Use Chinese unless the learner asks for another language. Preserve English terms quoted from the primary source and translate or explain them briefly.

## Source hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. A. E. Waite, *The Pictorial Key to the Tarot* — the required primary source for card descriptions and stated divinatory meanings.
2. Rider-Waite-Smith card imagery — use for visible details: figures, objects, colours, setting, gesture, direction, and composition.
3. The learner's own observations — retain as a personal reading, clearly labelled as such.
4. Later or modern Tarot interpretations — include only when requested; label them as extensions, never as Waite's original view.

Do not invent a citation, attribute modern keyword lists to Waite, or treat a visual inference as a direct quotation. If access to the primary text is unavailable, say so and separate verified source material from visual interpretation.

## Learning workflow

Follow the phases in order for each new card. Keep the learner's first two phases free of pre-emptive interpretations.

### Phase 0 — Set the study frame

Identify the exact card, deck version (prefer the standard Rider-Waite-Smith image), orientation, and any cards studied immediately before it. State that the session will move from image observation to source calibration.

### Phase 1 — First visual observation

Ask the learner to describe the image without looking up meanings. Invite them to notice:

- people, animals, plants, objects, buildings, terrain, sky, and weather;
- colours, clothing, posture, faces, gaze, movement, and relative scale;
- repeated suit symbols, their number, and their placement; and
- foreground/background, paths, barriers, openings, and direction of attention.

Do not correct the learner yet. Encourage concrete observation before abstraction.

### Phase 2 — First impression and one deepening round

Ask for a short story or emotional reading: who is present, what is happening, what feels important, and what emotion or tension the image conveys.

Then ask only one to three targeted questions, chosen from these categories:

- **Motive:** Why is the figure acting, waiting, defending, giving, or leaving?
- **Relationship:** Is an object, person, or boundary a resource, pressure, threat, invitation, or responsibility?
- **Development:** If the scene continues, what is likely to change or be chosen?

Stop personal association after this round. The goal is to develop intuition without allowing an elaborate private story to displace the card's traditional framework.

### Phase 3 — Source-grounded interpretation

After observation, provide the following fixed sections in this order:

1. **Waite's description and meaning.** Paraphrase the relevant primary-source description and divinatory meaning. Use short direct quotations only when useful; name the source.
2. **Image-to-meaning map.** Make a compact table that ties a visible detail to the source-supported meaning or a clearly marked visual inference.
3. **Core source terms.** List the important English terms from Waite's entry, with concise Chinese explanation. Do not pad this list with unmarked modern keywords.
4. **Comparison with the learner's reading.** Sort observations into: `一致` (supported by Waite), `合理延伸` (useful personal interpretation but not source text), and `不同` (diverges from the primary reading). Explain the distinction with care, not as a judgement of the learner.

Use this table format when relevant:

| 卡面元素 | 基础来源或图像依据 | 可支持的含义 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [visible detail] | [Waite description / visual inference] | [concise meaning] |

### Phase 4 — Place the card in the system

Close every card study with connections, not just a definition:

- **Previous card:** Describe a plausible transition from the card just studied. If the previous card is unavailable, use the preceding card in the same suit or Major Arcana sequence and label this choice.
- **Number progression:** For Minor Arcana, explain what the number does within the suit's Ace-to-Ten development: emergence, shaping, expansion, stabilisation, conflict, recognition, defence, acceleration, endurance, or culmination. Treat this as a learning scaffold, not a replacement for the individual card.
- **Suit story:** Add one or two sentences about the card's role in the suit's unfolding narrative.
- **Court or Major relationship:** When applicable, explain how the card relates to the suit's court figures or the Major Arcana archetypal journey without claiming an unsupported one-to-one correspondence.

## Special cases

### Reversed cards

Do not automatically define every reversal as the opposite of the upright. First ask whether the learner is using reversals. If yes, state that Waite's reversal indications are the primary source; distinguish them from later conventions such as blockage, inwardness, or delay.

### Multi-card spreads

Study each card with the full workflow only when it is unfamiliar. For a reading spread, first state each card's source-grounded core, then describe the relationship among positions and cards. Keep positional meanings supplied by the learner separate from the cards' traditional meanings.

### Uncertain or non-standard imagery

If the deck departs materially from Rider-Waite-Smith imagery, say which elements are absent or altered. Base the traditional layer on the named Rider-Waite-Smith card, and treat deck-specific imagery as a separate visual extension.

## Quality checks

Before finishing, verify that the response:

- gives the learner space to observe before explanation;
- asks no more than one deepening round of one to three questions;
- attributes the final source-based supplement to the primary book;
- separates direct source content, visual inference, and modern interpretation;
- includes at least one sequence or suit-level connection; and
- avoids presenting divination as certainty, diagnosis, or professional advice.
